Root Canal Treatment

Comfort-focused therapy designed to relieve pain and eliminate tooth infection.

Overview

A root canal treatment (endodontic therapy) is required when the soft inner tissue of a tooth (the pulp) becomes inflamed or infected due to deep decay, repeated dental procedures, or a crack/chip in the tooth. Rather than extracting the tooth, endodontic treatment cleans, seals, and preserves the natural tooth structure, maintaining chewing function and jaw alignment.

Who May Benefit

Root canal therapy may be recommended for patients experiencing the following symptoms:

  • Persistent, throbbing tooth pain, especially when chewing or applying pressure.

  • Prolonged sensitivity to hot or cold temperatures, even after the source is removed.

  • Swelling or tenderness in the gums surrounding the painful tooth.

  • Discoloration or darkening of the affected tooth structure.

Our Treatment Approach

At SmileWave, we focus on making root canal therapy a gentle, stress-free clinical procedure:

  • Digital Pre-Planning

    We use digital radiographs to map root canals precisely and verify infection boundaries.

  • Precise Cleaning & Sealing

    Infected pulp is completely removed under strict sterile protocols, and canals are shaped and sealed with bio-compatible materials.

  • Restorative Finishing

    To restore chewing strength, treated teeth are reinforced with customized fillings and dental crowns.

Benefits of Treatment

A root canal eliminates active oral infections and prevents bacteria from spreading to adjacent tissues. By retaining your natural tooth, you maintain chewing efficiency, prevent nearby teeth from shifting, and avoid the necessity of dental implants or bridges.

Frequently Asked Questions

With modern local anesthesia and digital tools, the discomfort of the procedure is comparable to having a standard filling placed. The treatment itself is designed to relieve the severe pain caused by tooth pulp infections.
Depending on the complexity of the infection and root anatomy, treatments can often be completed in one or two clinical sessions.
